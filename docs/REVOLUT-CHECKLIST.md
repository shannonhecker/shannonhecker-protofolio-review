# Revolut submission checklist

Source: Glenn's official brief supplied by Shannon on 10 October 2026. This is
an operational restatement, not a claim that Revolut has approved the portfolio.
Apply every criterion to **each** product page. Missing evidence is tracked
internally; never invent research, KPIs, adoption or shipped status to fill it.

## Requirements from the supplied brief

1. Show shipped, live mobile/web production features. The panel verifies live
   products and cross-references delivery timelines against LinkedIn.
2. Show individual UI craft and visual point of view even when working inside
   an established design system. Aim for a modern restrained aesthetic,
   intentional typography, generous spacing, hierarchy and semantic colour.
3. Include enough final high-resolution screens and visual explorations to judge
   the whole experience. Avoid isolated or zoomed snapshots without context.
4. Demonstrate complete end-to-end UX: primary navigation, native conventions,
   edge cases and validation states.
5. Begin concisely, then explain the problem and both user and business goals.
6. Explain constraints, blockers and iterations, including compliance and design
   system constraints. Keep the work problem-led.
7. Show decisions and trade-offs: alternatives X/Y, what was tested, chosen X
   and the reason Z. Make exploration and thought process visible.
8. Show outcomes: what shipped, impact, adoption and KPIs. Provide depth through
   data-driven testing and impact metrics rather than unsupported assertions.
9. Demonstrate recency, exceptional attention to detail and simple frictionless UX.
10. Check everything before the single submission opportunity. Supply an updated
    CV if needed and ensure dates agree across portfolio, CV and LinkedIn.

Shannon requires all points on every case. Stage-appropriate disclosure is
necessary but does not turn an alpha into a shipped production product. Equal
visual prominence does not imply equal maturity or evidence strength.
